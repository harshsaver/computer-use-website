What makes you, you?

December studies a precise question: what must persist for a person to remain the same person through change?

We will test it inside a continuous world where artificial residents develop bodies, memories, relationships, and complete life histories.

Folio I Research question

Primary question

Can identity survive a change of substrate?

Human identity appears continuous even as the body, memories, and beliefs change. We want to identify which structures and causal histories produce that continuity.

Research boundary

December does not assume that its residents are conscious. It does not claim to have solved consciousness, uploading, or immortality.

Folio III Research protocol

Method

Causality before spectacle.

  1. 01

    Build the causal world

    Prove conservation, time, health, ecology, replay, and failure recovery without AI residents.

    Kernel
  2. 02

    Add bounded minds

    Give residents private observations and memory. AI proposes actions; the world decides outcomes.

    Cognition
  3. 03

    Run complete histories

    Let projects, relationships, institutions, migration, aging, and death unfold without a storyteller.

    Longitudinal
  4. 04

    Intervene only in copies

    Replay and fork the same life, change one cause, and measure when identity or behavior diverges.

    Counterfactual
Folio IV Evidence standard

What would count as progress?

A result must survive inspection.

A compelling story is not evidence. December will publish tests, failures, and uncertainty with every major claim.

  • Replay

    The same inputs reproduce the same material history.

  • Lineage

    Every outcome links to prior state, decisions, and random draws.

  • Privacy

    Residents cannot act on information they never received.

  • Continuity

    Identity remains stable across context resets and approved model changes.

  • Ablation

    Removing memory, relationships, or scarcity measurably changes lives.

  • Humility

    Results are simulation findings, not claims about real human populations.
