01 · The question

Your body changes.

Your memories change.

Your beliefs change.

Yet you remain you.

What creates that sense of one continuous life? We do not know. December is a new way to explore the question.

03 · Why a whole world?

A mind does not
exist alone.

A person is shaped by more than thoughts. Bodies, memories, other people, loss, work, culture, and time all become part of a life. To understand a person, we need to understand the world around them.

Body

Hunger, health, age, effort, and death are real constraints.

Memory

People remember imperfectly. What they believe can be wrong.

Other people

Trust, love, promises, conflict, and care change who we become.

Time

Choices accumulate. Nothing important is quietly reset.

04 · What we hope to learn
  1. 01

    How identity persists while a person changes.

  2. 02

    How memory and relationships shape the self.

  3. 03

    How a continuous life may give rise to consciousness.

  4. 04

    What would need to persist for a person to continue.

05 · The distant hope

Could a life continue
beyond the body?

We do not know. December is not a promise of immortality. It begins with a smaller, harder question: can we understand a human life well enough to know what must be preserved?

If that becomes possible, a life may not have to end where biology does.

06 · What is true today
Today

We have a plan.

The world and its rules are being designed and independently audited.

Next

We build and test.

We start with the physical world, then memory, relationships, and minds.

Someday

We may understand more.

Consciousness and immortality remain questions—not claims.
